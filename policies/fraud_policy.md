# Synthetic Fraud Review Policy

> Demonstration policy only. It is not legal, regulatory, or banking advice.

## High Transaction Velocity

Multiple transactions over a short period may indicate compromised credentials or automated card testing. Review velocity together with amount, device, merchant, and customer history.

## Unusual Transaction Amount

An amount materially above a customer's prior spending can increase risk. Amount alone is insufficient; compare it with history and corroborating signals.

## International Activity

An international transaction is not fraud by itself. Risk increases when the geography differs from prior behavior and appears with other anomalies.

## Geographic Anomaly

A first-seen or unexpected country warrants review when combined with unusual amount, timing, velocity, or device evidence.

## New Device

A previously unseen device may indicate account takeover, but legitimate device replacement is common. Seek additional evidence before escalation.

## Card-Not-Present Risk

Online and mobile transactions have different exposure than card-present purchases. Channel should modify risk, never determine fraud alone.

## Unusual Transaction Hours

Overnight activity can be relevant when it is inconsistent with customer history. Time of day is a weak signal unless supported by other evidence.

## Merchant-Category Change

A first-seen merchant category can be suspicious when its value or context differs materially from prior behavior.

## Account Takeover Indicators

A combination of new device, new geography, rapid activity, and unusual amount may indicate account takeover and should receive prompt analyst attention.

## Escalation Requirements

Escalate when multiple corroborating indicators and the model score exceed the approved review threshold. Record the evidence and cited policy sections.

## Analyst Review

Human analysts validate context, contact customers when authorized, and choose any high-impact action. The investigation assistant only recommends.

## High-Risk Transaction Handling

High-risk alerts should be prioritized by score and evidence strength. A high score is not proof of fraud and must remain subject to human review.

## Evidence Quality

Recommendations must rely on tool-returned facts and retrieved policy text. Unsupported claims must not be included in the case summary.

## False Positive Safeguard

Legitimate travel, major purchases, and device changes can look unusual. Analysts should consider benign explanations before adverse action.

## Audit Trail

Log tool calls, evidence, policy citations, recommendation, and final human disposition so outcomes can support review and monitoring.
